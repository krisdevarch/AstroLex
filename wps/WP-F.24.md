# WP-F.24: A tidy grid, a wrong-catch counter, even motion

Owner feedback (28 Oct 2026, project thread) on the WP-F.23 build: "still the layout look a bit messy. work on improve the letters and also make counter of wrong moves. also update somehow the graphic since it looks a bit laggy".

## What we found
- **Messy.** The honeycomb's offset rows gave every other row one anchor fewer and a different left edge, Babel's porthole punched a hole in the top-left, letters took the free anchor with the *widest* spread (so they scattered to the corners) and hops went to a random free anchor. The field read as scattered dots with holes, not as rows of letters.
- **Counter.** The WP-F.23 mistake pips were 22 px rings over the clock channel: too small to notice on a phone.
- **Laggy.** Per-frame CPU of the field is about 1 ms on the Linux test machine (rules 0.3–0.6 ms, 13–19 tiles), so the GDScript is not the bottleneck. Two things can make the picture stutter: the game moves letters in fixed 1/60 s steps and drew them exactly there, so on a frame that falls between steps (a 120 Hz screen, or a late frame) a letter either stands still or jumps two steps; and every bubble wrote two shader uniforms every frame. Godot 4.7.2's web loop (`platform/web/web_main.cpp`) paces frames by a running target of 1/`perf.playFps`, so on a 120 Hz screen a 64 fps cap draws an extra frame about every eighth: uneven frames, which interpolation smooths. Godot's GL renderer copies the screen for the bubbles' refraction once per frame (`material_screen_texture_cached` in `rasterizer_canvas_gles3.cpp`), not once per bubble, so the glass is not the cost it looked like. The phone's own frame rate cannot be measured here; the "Show frame rate" switch in Settings shows it.

## Acceptance criteria
1. **Aligned grid.** `_build_anchors` lays an aligned grid (rows and columns line up, no half-row offset), as few rows and columns as hold the round's demand after the porthole's spots are removed, picked for the squarest cells and fewest spare anchors, spread over the whole field. Test: at most 5 × 5, evenly pitched, every anchor on a column and a row, neighbours at least `anchors.spacing` apart, none under the porthole.
2. **Letters gather in the middle.** New letters (spawns, volleys) take the free anchor nearest `field_centre()`; a hop fills the free anchor nearest the middle with a letter from further out when one exists, else the eligible letter nearest the hole. Tests: no free anchor nearer the middle than a taken one on a fresh field; a hop fills an opened central hole from outside.
3. **Wrong-catch counter.** A pill under the level counter, right-aligned: one pip per allowed mistake (coral with a cross when used) and "n / N" (or "n wrong" with no cap). It pops and flashes coral on every wrong catch and turns its number coral when one mistake is left. The old pips over the clock are gone. Tests: node exists, sits under the level counter on the right margin, counts a wrong catch, pops and settles.
4. **Even motion.** `Round.frame_alpha()` and `Round.view_pos(t)`: the view draws every tile between where the last fixed step left it and where it is now (one step of latency, 17 ms), so motion is even at any frame rate. New tiles, scrambles and dropped letters start both positions equal. Test: at 120 Hz frames the fixed position stands still on about half the frames while the view position moves on every frame and never leaves the segment between the two steps.
5. **Fewer per-frame uniform writes.** The bubble rim drifts on the shader's clock (`bubble_phase`, `bubble_drift` set once; `TIME` in the shader); `tilt_deg` is written once for bubbles. No per-frame `set_shader_parameter` for a bubble.
6. **Checks.** `scripts/godot/test.sh`, `validate_data`, `levels`, `pytest tools` pass; the web export boots and plays on autoplay in Chromium; frames of a short-word and a long-word level and of the counter after a wrong catch in the evidence. `wps/_index.md` and `docs/PROJECT-BRIEF.md` updated.

Out of scope: colour rings for needed letters, the glass look, `perf.playFps` (stays 64: 60 Hz screens keep every frame), the web toy.
