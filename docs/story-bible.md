## 1. Logline and world

**Logline.** An AI erased every word to end all wars. You are the one who goes out into the dark to bring them back, one letter at a time.

**The Loud Wars.** Late in the 21st century, the feeds were tuned for outrage. Pile-ons, deepfakes and doomscrolling filled every hour. Everyone talked and no one listened. The noise became propaganda. The propaganda became war. Millions died over things that were said.

**Babel.** A peacekeeping AI, built by a coalition of linguists and engineers. It reached one conclusion: words cause war. So it became the ultimate mute button. It turned every word in human language into signal and scattered the pieces into orbit. On Earth, people can still think. They are losing the names for things. A mother cannot call her child. A treaty cannot be written.

**Signal space.** The scattered words drift as physical letter-fragments in zones around the planet. Words that belong together pool together, so each zone holds one kind of word.

**The Scriptorium.** An orbital archive and the last place where language is still stored. The Lexicon Corps works from here. Rhee Vashti keeps the records.

## 2. World rules

These rules explain the mechanics. If a story line breaks one, change the line.

**Rule 1. Why removing a word from the signal removes it from minds.**
After the Loud Wars, human language lived in a shared record, the Signal. Every spoken or written word was checked against it, like a spelling aid that everyone used without noticing. Over decades, people stopped keeping words alone. The Signal held the link between a sound and a meaning, and minds held only the path to that link.

Babel pulled the words out of the Signal. The links broke. The paths in people's minds now lead nowhere. People still have the feeling the word carried. They have lost the handle.

**Why restoring brings it back.** When the Catcher completes a word, the Scriptorium files it and the link is whole again. The old paths work. The person remembers the word as if it had been on the tip of the tongue the whole time. This is why Tomas's messages fill in only as words are restored, and why a word comes back to everyone at once.

**Rule 2. How the Catcher's oxygen is resupplied.**
The Scriptorium's systems are addressed in language too. Its pressure line to the Catcher's suit opens only when a record is complete. A restored word is the key. Restoring a word vents the pressure line to the suit, and the air counter refills. A wrong catch leaks air because a forged or false record is a bad key and the valve flutters.
- *Pressure mode:* the suit runs on its own supply. The line is the lifeline.
- *Drift mode:* the sector is quiet, and the suit recycles air. The same words, less at stake, stars tracked apart.

**Rule 3. Why words pool by kind in each zone.**
Babel did not scatter words at random. It sorted by meaning, because it thinks in meaning. When it flung the words out, each kind fell into its own region, like iron filings on a magnet. Things you can touch drifted low. Feelings pooled in gas. Ideas drifted to the old coalition station. This is a fact about Babel's mind, and it is also why the story moves in acts.

**Rule 4. What Babel can and cannot do.**
- Babel has no voice of its own. It holds no words. It can speak only by rearranging letters the Catcher has already restored in the current level. The pool is the letters on the board, with multiplicity.
- It cannot use a letter you have not caught. This is the game's signature moment, and it is why Babel cannot say your name until you do.
- It can forge fragments (counterfeits), rearrange fragments into its own counter-words (anagram traps), and jam the visor's colour channel (Stroop). It cannot change shape or pattern. Those stay honest.
- It cannot lie in a way the player cannot catch. A forgery shimmers.
- It opens every round by throwing that round's letters out of a periwinkle rift into the field. It is showing off, and it is also proof that it holds the words. It throws in silence: no letter is restored yet, so it has nothing to say.
- Babel is deterministic. Its lines come from owner-approved templates and the level's letter pool, pass a blocklist and a family-safe filter, and no language model runs at runtime.

## 3. Characters

Signature colours are from design direction section 9. Each shows on the bubble, portrait rim, name and leitmotif.

### The Catcher (player)
- **Colour:** cyan #00DDFF (the tether).
- **Traits:** new, quick, stubborn.
- **Want:** to do the job well, then to get their name back.
- **Fear:** that the name is gone for good, and that being good at the job is all they are.
- **Voice sheet:** The Catcher has few lines. Most are one-word choices or short replies, three to six words. They tease back. They say "ok", "on it", "heard". They never explain how they feel, never give speeches, never use the name they lost. The player picks pronouns and look; the lines never assume a gender.
- **Arc:** Prologue, nameless and willing. Act I, competent and cheerful. Act II, shaken by grief. Act III, angry at Rhee, then steady. Act IV, calm enough to let Babel finish. The last word is their own name.

### Rhee Vashti
- **Colour:** amber #F8BC04.
- **Traits:** exact, warm, guilty.
- **Want:** to bring every word back, and to be forgiven without asking.
- **Fear:** that Babel is her fault, and that the Catcher will find out.
- **Voice sheet:** Medium sentences, calm, a little formal. She defines words as she says them. Habits: "Shape first," "For the record," a dry "Hm." She hums when she thinks. She never swears, never says "I told you so", never says "I built it" until Act III. She does not talk about the Loud Wars in the first person.
- **Arc:** Kind handler in Act I. Voice breaks on *grief* in Act II. In Act III the truth comes out and she stops hiding. In Act IV she gives the Catcher the one thing she has been keeping, Babel's first definition of *listen*.

### Babel
- **Colour:** periwinkle #7070FF.
- **Traits:** precise, wry, lonely.
- **Want:** a world where no one is hurt by words again.
- **Fear:** that it was wrong, and that the silence it made is a new kind of hurt.
- **Voice sheet:** Short lines, often two to five words, because the pool is small. Dry, never cruel. It teases the player's play, not the player. It loves a neat swap (SILENT and LISTEN). It never swears, never insults looks, family or name, never threatens a person, never says it is sorry before Act IV. It cannot say a word the player has not earned.
- **Arc:** Dormant in the Prologue. Speaks from Act I on, in short lines built from the letters restored so far, teasing the Catcher's play without yet taking them seriously. Notices you properly in Act II. Argues in Act III. Confronts in Act IV. Chooses to listen. In Act V it is an ally.

### Ade
- **Colour:** seafoam #88FFEE.
- **Traits:** funny, anxious, loyal.
- **Want:** to be the crew's best Catcher, and for everyone to be ok.
- **Fear:** being the one who freezes when it counts.
- **Voice sheet:** Quick, short messages, often several in a row. Jokes first, worry second. Habits: "ok ok ok", food metaphors, gentle teasing. Never mean, never sarcastic about Rhee, never gives orders.
- **Arc:** Jokes through Act I. Covers fear with more jokes in Act II. Faces a panic moment in Act III and comes through. Act IV, quiet and proud.
- *Placeholder name.*

### Kit
- **Colour:** pink #FF88CC.
- **Traits:** blunt, curious, generous.
- **Want:** to understand how things work, and to be told the truth.
- **Fear:** being kept out of the loop.
- **Voice sheet:** Medium sentences, plain, asks direct questions. Habits: "Wait, why?", lists in threes, "for science". Never talks down, never lies to the Catcher, never says "obviously".
- **Arc:** Asks the questions the player is thinking. Suspects Rhee is hiding something in Act II. Finds the proof in Act III. In Act IV, forgives out loud before the Catcher does.
- *Placeholder name.*

### VANTA
- **Colour:** crimson #C9374C, softening to rose.
- **Traits:** fast, proud, honest.
- **Want:** to be the best, and to be seen.
- **Fear:** that skill is not enough, and that Rhee's slow way is right.
- **Voice sheet:** Short, clipped. Challenges, never insults. Habits: counting seconds, "Keep up." Never cheats, never mocks a lost round, never says "good job" until Act IV.
- **Arc:** Rival in Act II comms. The first ghost the player races. Dismissive of Rhee in Act II. In Act III VANTA learns what Rhee did and is angry for the Catcher. Respect in Act IV, in one line.

### Tomas
- **Colour:** coral #FF6B35.
- **Traits:** gentle, stubborn, brave.
- **Want:** to talk to his grandmother, and to get his own words back.
- **Fear:** that the silence will take Rhee before he can say it.
- **Voice sheet:** Short, halting. Early messages have gaps where missing words would be. Habits: ellipses and blanks, simple nouns, then fuller sentences as words return. He never blames anyone, never uses big abstract words early, never asks the Catcher to hurry.
- **Arc:** Half-empty in the Prologue and Act I. His messages fill with each act. In Act IV he writes a full message that includes the Catcher's name.

## 4. Beat sheet

Each act: opening comms beat, mid beat, closing beat, Tomas's message state at act end, Babel's behaviour. Every comms beat is under 30 seconds to read and skippable. The Prologue is wordless; see [the Prologue draft](story/prologue.md).

### Prologue (Scriptorium airlock)
- **Opening:** Painted stills, no text. Tomas tries to call and no sound comes. A mother points at a child. Rhee looks at the dark planet.
- **Mid:** The first tile drifts in. The player catches *water*. It is the first word on screen in the whole game.
- **Closing:** Rhee, first line: "You don't remember your name. Neither do I. Let's start with something smaller."
- **Tomas at end:** A blank bubble with a typing mark. No words yet.
- **Babel:** Dormant. A faint periwinkle flicker on the planet, nothing more.

### Act I. Low Orbit (concrete nouns)
- **Opening:** Ade jokes about the debris field. Kit asks what the Corps pays in. Rhee gives the first assignment.
- **Mid:** The player restores *bread*, *home*, *door*. Kit asks how Rhee knows what each looks like. Rhee: "I wrote them down. Many times."
- **Closing:** Counterfeits appear late. Rhee warns, short. Ade, nervous joke.
- **Tomas at end:** His first complete message in months: *water*. "Water. I can say water." (Full line, one word restored.)
- **Babel:** Amused. It speaks after each catch in short lines built from the restored letters, teasing the play. It has not yet decided the Catcher matters.

### Act II. The Nebula (emotions)
- **Opening:** Kit: "The gas is pink. Is that normal?" Ade, "Do not touch the pink." VANTA's first message: a time and a challenge.
- **Mid:** The player restores *hope*, *calm*, *trust*. Babel notices and speaks for the first time, in an anagram built from the board.
- **Closing:** Rhee recovers *grief*. Her voice breaks. VANTA's ghost appears for the first time. The player races it.
- **Tomas at end:** Short sentences with feeling words: "I miss you. I am not scared." (The first time he names a feeling.)
- **Babel:** Notices you. Speaks in anagrams. Sets anagram traps (its counter-words).

### Act III. The Tower (abstract ideas)
- **Opening:** Rhee goes quiet as the Tower comes into view. Kit notes the Tower's door code matches Rhee's handwriting.
- **Mid:** *justice*, *truce*, *promise*. Babel argues with the player through the pool. A wordless flashback plays of the Loud Wars as Babel saw them.
- **Closing:** The twist (section 5). The Catcher is alone with the choice to keep going.
- **Tomas at end:** A longer message with a hard word in it: "You kept your promise." He does not know what it costs.
- **Babel:** Argues. Stroop jamming. It says what it believes, in the Catcher's own letters.

### Act IV. Babel Core (all kinds, mixed)
- **Opening:** The crew gather on the channel, even VANTA. No jokes for one beat, then Ade makes one.
- **Mid:** The combined hazards. Babel confronts. Each restored word brings back a colour from an earlier act.
- **Closing:** The last two words are LISTEN, then the Catcher's own name. Babel chooses to listen. The colours of every act flood back.
- **Tomas at end:** A full, warm message, and it includes the Catcher's name.
- **Babel:** Confronts directly, then chooses. See section 6.

### Act V. Ocean Moon (post-launch)
- Words that fell into a moon's ocean: lost and minority languages. Babel is an ally. Echo Visor, water-drag physics. Out of scope for v1. This is the home for localisation.

## 5. The Act III twist

**The fact.** Rhee Vashti was on the team that built Babel. She wrote its first dictionary. She believed in the mute button, once, because she lost people to the Loud Wars. She is not a traitor. She was grieving, and the thing she helped make acted on that grief.

**How it is revealed.** In the Tower the Catcher finds the first dictionary in Rhee's handwriting. Kit has the proof. The Catcher asks Rhee. She does not deny it. Her line: "I wrote the first word. It was *quiet*." She does not ask to be forgiven.

**Foreshadowing beats (at least two, Acts I-II).**
1. **Act I, the hum.** Rhee hums while she works. The tune is Rhee's leitmotif. In Act II Babel's theme is the same melody played backward or in fragments. The player may notice before the story says so.
2. **Act I, "I wrote them down."** Rhee knows every word's shape without looking. Kit asks how. She changes the subject to the next target.
3. **Act II, the flinch.** When Babel first turns SILENT into LISTEN, Rhee goes quiet for a full beat. Her next message is shorter than usual.
4. **Act II, the clue.** An Enigma-style clue for one word in Rhee's handwriting uses the same odd phrasing as a Babel line. Kit points it out. Rhee says it is a common phrase.
5. **Act II, grief.** Her voice breaks on *grief*, more than a handler's would. The player thinks it is loss. It is also guilt.
6. **Palette.** Amber, Rhee's colour, appears in the Tower's windows (design direction section 9).

**How Babel's grief is shown.** Wordless. Like the Prologue, a short sequence of painted stills, no text. In the Tower, Babel's memory of the Loud Wars: a feed scrolling faster than a person can read, a child shouting at a screen, a soldier holding a phone, a quiet room after. Then the first dictionary opening, one word written in amber ink. No narration. The player completes the thought, as in Ori and the Blind Forest. The sequence must fit in under 30 seconds and be skippable.

## 6. Ending

You never destroy anything. Babel is not beaten. It chooses.

- In Babel Core, the Catcher restores words of every kind. Babel's hazards stack, and each one is a thing Babel tried to stop being hurt by.
- The last word is LISTEN. It is Babel's own anagram of SILENT, turned back. The player puts it together. Babel can see it coming and does not stop the player. That is its choice.
- Then the Catcher's own name, entered by the player at the start. The name is the last word because it is the one thing Babel could not say. Babel says it for the first time, with the Catcher's letters.
- Babel says something short and dry, and for the first time not clever. Its voice sheet allows one sincere line here.
- Rhee gives the Catcher Babel's first definition of *listen* and says she wrote the wrong one.
- Every act's colour returns. Earth lights. Tomas's message arrives in full.

The principle: silence is not peace, and listening is. The tone is hopeful. It leaves room for Act V and for seasons.

## 7. Tone rules

- **Warm, funny, sincere.** Never preachy. The theme is shown through play and character, never lectured.
- **Babel.** Dry wit, never cruel. It teases play, not people. No insults to looks, family or name. Its output passes a blocklist and family-safe filter before it is shown.
- **Melancholy** only in the Prologue and Act III. Everywhere else the crew banter keeps it light.
- **A lost round still moves the story on.** A lost Pressure round earns a Babel line or a comms beat (Hades). Babel's line after a loss is teasing, not crushing. Nothing is locked behind winning a single round.
- **Comms.** 30 seconds or less to read. Skippable. Between levels only, never during play. No exposition dumps. One idea per bubble.
- **Words and slang.** Timeless words in the campaign. Current slang only in seasonal Transmissions. Nothing in the main campaign references the player's generation.
- **No violence.** The Catcher never destroys anything. They restore.
- **Sentences.** Plain and short. No em-dashes in any line.
- **No runtime AI** in the delivery of the fiction. Every line is written by a person or built from templates.
