# AstroLex: Market and Audience Assessment

> **Purpose:** records whether AstroLex makes sense commercially, how it can make money, and why Draft 3 of the plan targets millennials first and older Gen Z second.
> **Date:** September 2026. **Method:** desk research through web search. Sensor Tower and AppMagic pages couldn't be opened directly, so their figures come from search-result summaries. Treat every number as approximate, and re-check it before using it in a budget (`BIZ-001`).

---

## 1. Verdict

- **It makes sense to build.** The concept is original. It has a clear identity, a shareable hook (Babel's anagram voice, and your own name as the last word) and a timely theme. It's also cheap to produce with an owner plus AI agents.
- **It can make money, but probably not as a free-to-play hit.** The realistic model is a **hybrid**: a free start, a one-time campaign unlock, and a daily-puzzle habit loop that sells season passes and cosmetics. A platform deal is a strong alternative (§4).
- **The first proof points are cheap:** the Phase 1 fun test, and the Phase 2 two-audience fake-door test.

## 3. Risks

1. **Audience fit.** Word-game spenders lean older and female ([about 75% of US word and puzzle players are women](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/modern-mobile-gamer)), and they play relaxed sessions. AstroLex adds a clock, aiming and hazards. Draft 3's answer is to pick the audience (§5) and add a relaxed *Drift* mode.
2. **Few money levers.** Puzzle games earn mostly from lives, energy and boosters, and [a small share of payers brings in most of the revenue](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/). AstroLex's ethics rule these out, so revenue per player will be lower. Hence the campaign unlock.
3. **User acquisition cost.** Puzzle installs cost [about $3 on iOS and $2 on Android](https://www.businessofapps.com/ads/cpi/research/cost-per-install/). Growth has to come from sharing, press and featuring, not from paid ads alone.
4. **The AI irony.** A game built with AI agents *about* an AI that erased language may draw criticism. Mitigation: O-14 requires human-made shipped art and audio, and the team should say so openly.

## 5. Audience: why millennials first, older Gen Z second

- **Millennials (about 27–40 in 2026)** are already at the core word-game spending age. Adults 25–44 are [the biggest spenders on mobile](https://huntervault.app/blog/mobile-gaming-spending-by-age/). They play daily puzzles and story games, and they pay for fair one-time purchases.
- **Older Gen Z (about 18–26)** bring reach. Word puzzles are ["cool" again with Gen Z](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/62601/1/are-puzzles-cool-now-gen-z-nyt-connections-strands-mini-wordle), a [puzzle-creator scene exists on TikTok](https://www.ypulse.com/article/2024/07/22/new-creator-genres-tiktoks-puzzle-players/), and [58% of Gen Z call gaming their main social activity](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/gen-z-gaming-platform-preferences-statistics/). They spend on self-expression ([study](https://www.gamerbraves.com/study-finds-gen-z-and-millennials-spend-money-in-games-for-very-different-reasons/)) but less per head, and [prefer free-to-play](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/gen-z-gaming-platform-preferences-statistics/).
- **Both groups** play in short sessions and socialise in games. [40% of Gen Z and millennials socialise more in games than in person](https://www.adjust.com/blog/social-gaming-gen-z/).
- **Teens are not targeted.** The rating stays 13+, but marketing aims at 18+. That avoids most of the extra safety and privacy work.

### What changed in the plan (Draft 3)

| Area | Change |
|------|--------|
| Story | The Loud Wars become internet-era noise (feeds, pile-ons, deepfakes). The Catcher is a young recruit with a crew (Ade, Kit) and a rival (VANTA). Babel is witty and quotable. Comms are chat messages. Slang appears only in seasons. |
| Game | *Drift* and *Pressure* modes, a Daily Signal share card, pronouns and a visible, customisable Catcher, music-led feel |
| Social | Accounts, the Daily Signal, friend ghosts, daily duels and safety basics move to Phase 3. No open chat. |
| Money | Hybrid model (§4) |
| Proof | A two-audience fake-door test at the Phase 2 gate. Sharing targets at the Phase 7 gate. |
| Cost | About 4–5 extra weeks before launch |

## 6. How to validate (cheap, before big spend)

1. **Phase 1:** the greybox fun test with at least 3 millennial and at least 2 older-Gen-Z testers. Record which mode they choose.
2. **Phase 2:** the fake-door test, with two store pages and two small short-video ad sets ("cozy word mystery" against "Babel roasts your spelling"). Compare click-through and cost per install by age band. The budget is a few hundred dollars.
3. **Phase 3:** a friends-and-family social test. Target: at least 30% of Daily Signal completions shared.
4. **Phase 7:** soft-launch gates for retention, campaign-unlock conversion and sharing (`BIZ-002`).
