# AstroLex: Market Evidence Review (September 2026)

> **What this is.** Desk research, done on 29 September 2026, into what has made small-team word and puzzle games succeed or fail from 2022 to 2026, and what that means for AstroLex. It backs §4 of `AstroLex-Repo-Analysis-and-Path-to-Success.md` and supersedes the numbers in `docs/market/AstroLex-Market-and-Audience.md` where they differ.
> **Method note.** About 60 web searches over 2025–2026 sources. Direct fetches to several primary domains (Sensor Tower, GameAnalytics, Game Developer, Quantic Foundry) were blocked by the session proxy, so some figures come from search extracts and secondary coverage. Single-source or low-credibility figures are marked. Where nothing was found, that is stated.

---

## 1. What has made small-team word and puzzle games succeed, 2022–2026

**The pattern.** The wins are overwhelmingly either (a) free, web-first daily puzzles that spread through a share artefact and were then licensed or acquired by a media company, or (b) low-friction, no-timer, big-portfolio free-to-play word games with ad monetisation and paid user acquisition. Nothing in the sample succeeded as a stand-alone premium or unlock-based mobile word game without a platform patron (Apple Arcade, App Store featuring) or a pre-existing name.

Notes on the most relevant cases:

- **Wordle.** The emoji grid was added *after* players began hand-posting emoji blocks. About 23.5M score tweets by 2.1M unique users were posted from December 2021 to February 2022.
- **Alphabear** is the closest analogue to AstroLex's signature feature. Its virality came from "bear speech": auto-generated, often inappropriate sentences built from the player's words, which people shared *because* they were inappropriate. Reach was enormous and revenue was not. Spry Fox was later bought by Netflix (2022) and sold back to its founders (December 2025), who cut their salaries to $20K a year.
- **NYT added a timer to Connections: Sports Edition in February 2025, as an option.** Timers remain opt-in in the largest word franchise.
- **Puzzmo** launched behind a 500-keys-per-day waitlist; 104K people made 932K attempts to get in. Free daily puzzles account for "nearly an hour of gameplay a day" among engaged users.
- **Knotwords and SpellTower** show even a well-known designer moving from paid-up-front to free-to-start plus a small unlock, and then to Apple Arcade. Neither has published numbers.
- **Babble Royale** is the clearest data point that real-time competitive word play is a tiny niche, even with a famous designer.
- **Wordle-alike launches** fell from 46 in 2022 to 2 in 2024.

## 2. Economics of a one-time-unlock hybrid on mobile, 2025–2026

- **Demo-to-unlock conversion: no public word-game data exists.** No developer has disclosed free-to-start to unlock conversion for a word game. Generic guidance is that 2–5% of players pay in F2P. The nearest hard proxies are from Steam: median demo-to-wishlist 16.3%, median wishlist-to-day-one sale about 12%. A rough inference for a mobile free-to-start funnel (about 30% D1, times finishing the free act, times 3–8% of finishers buying) lands at roughly 1–2% of installs converting. That is an assumption to test, not a benchmark.
- **Premium is growing in count, not share.** Premium mobile releases were up 77% in 2025 to about 750 titles, while F2P still took 96% of downloads (AppMagic). Balatro, the reference premium mobile success, made about $500–550K in its first five days on iOS and Android, on the back of 2M+ prior PC and console sales. No example was found of the unlock model working cold for an unknown word game.
- **Revenue per user.** Puzzle ARPDAU is about $0.08 (AppsFlyer 2025); casual $0.05–0.15; hybrid-casual blended $0.15–0.50. Puzzle is roughly 80% ad-funded as a genre.
- **Retention.** GameAnalytics 2025 (11,600 games): puzzle about 32% D1, 12% D7, 5% D30. GameAnalytics 2026: all-genre median D1 about 22%, D7 just under 4%; top 10% D1 about 40%; top 1% D1 64–68%. **No word-genre-specific retention benchmark has been published since 2022.** Frictionless daily products (LinkedIn: 84% D1) sit far above these medians.
- **Cost per install.** Puzzle about $3.00 iOS and $2.00 Android (Business of Apps 2025); all-genre averages $4.22 iOS and $2.97 Android (2026). With puzzle ARPDAU near $0.08 and a $5–8 unlock at an assumed 1–2% conversion, paid UA at $2–3 CPI loses money. The plan's "no paid UA" is correct, not merely a constraint.

## 3. Discovery without paid user acquisition

- **App Store featuring.** Since iOS 18, Featuring Nominations in App Store Connect are the formal channel (App Launch, New Content, App Enhancements). Apple asks for at least two weeks' lead, up to three months for launches. Editors reward platform features (haptics, iCloud, widgets, Game Center, accessibility) and a credible launch plan. Apple Design Award finalists and App Store Award winners are the ones who then get Arcade "+" offers. Vendor claims of about 800% download uplift for Game of the Day are unaudited. For this design, featuring is the single best free channel, and it is a lottery you enter, not a plan.
- **Google Play.** The Indie Games Fund is regional and equity-free (Latin America 2025, Africa 2026). The Indie Games Festival has not run since 2022. Featuring for a solo Western developer is rare.
- **Apple Arcade.** Invite-only. Upfront fees and bonus-pool payouts have reportedly shrunk since 2020, with payment delays. 2026 additions favour large IP and award winners. The path for an indie is ship, get featured or awarded, then be approached. Not a launch channel.
- **Netflix.** Effectively closed to indies in 2026: 20+ licensed games removed in July 2025, 13 more in December 2025, Spry Fox divested, Night School and Moonloot closed in August 2026. Netflix Puzzled is a free rival.
- **Steam.** Puzzle is the most over-supplied, lowest-earning genre: 4,022 puzzle releases in 2025, and only 14 (0.34%) reached 1,000 reviews. Next Fest 2025 median gain was 806 wishlists; games entering with under 1,000 wishlists gained a median of 462. A Steam page is a cheap validation surface, not a revenue plan for a mobile-first word game.
- **Web-first.** The only channel with multiple small-team breakouts in the period (Wordle, Puzzmo, Clues by Sam, Bracket City). All were free, daily, instantly playable, and had a spoiler-free share artefact. Media companies (NYT, Hearst, The Atlantic, Apple News+ with Emoji Game in July 2025, Netflix Puzzled) are actively acquiring and licensing daily games, which makes a web "Daily Signal" both a growth engine and an exit option.
- **TikTok and Shorts.** Works for games with a five-second visual hook. Bacon: The Game hit 160K downloads in one day off a 9M-view TikTok and 10M+ iOS downloads with about 10K organic installs a day. Zero-G 3D letters and an anagram-speaking antagonist are visually legible in a way most word games are not.
- **Share cards.** Wordle is the only well-documented case. No public share-rate data exists for Connections or Strands. Alphabear's bear speech is the cautionary twin: massive shares, weak monetisation.
- **Discord and Reddit playtests.** Guidance: 20–50 testers in a first wave, 50–200 later; under 30% unprompted second-session return signals a core-loop problem.

## 4. Audience reality check

- **Who plays and pays.** Women dominate word-game engagement (Newzoo MGI 2024). Mobile's revenue core is US women 35+, with the strongest cohorts at 35–44 and 55+ (Appodeal 2026). The average US player is 37, and 65+ is the largest single US age bracket at 22% of players (ESA 2025). The NYT says Gen Z and millennials are its fastest-growing Games cohort. So the plan's 27–40 target is real, but it is not where word-game *spend* concentrates. That is 35+ and female-skewing.
- **Reflex and timing fit.** Quantic Foundry (250K+ gamers): competition is the motivation that declines most with age (top at 13–25, ninth at 36+); excitement and challenge are the least common primary motivations over 36; completion and strategy are age-stable. Among action-word hybrids, the commercially notable one (Bookworm Adventures) was turn-based; Babble Royale (real-time) is dead; Spellspire got mixed reviews; typing games like Epistory sold 100K+ but on PC to a keyboard audience. Netflix Boggle Party and SpellTower Blitz make timers optional. Alphabear used decaying tiles (soft pressure) and still reached 10M players. **Conclusion:** soft pressure is tolerated; hard reflex gates are not evidenced anywhere in the successful word set. An oxygen clock as the default is a mismatch with the paying demographic. As an optional difficulty layer over a relaxed default, it is defensible.

## 5. Validation techniques and "go" thresholds

- **Fake-door and ad click-through tests.** Generic guidance: 2–5% CTR viable, under 1% rethink. TikTok conversion-optimised CTR averages 0.84%. For AstroLex: a $200–500 TikTok or Meta test on three concept videos, with a "go" at CTR of 1.5–2% or better and a mock-install CPI under about $1 on US iOS. Anything worse confirms organic-only, which is the plan anyway.
- **Web prototype virality.** Clues by Sam's own bar: several failed attempts before one stuck; the signal was unpaid week-over-week growth plus a streamer spike. A "go" for a web Daily Signal is sustained week-over-week growth without promotion and a measurable share rate.
- **Steam page as a wishlist barometer.** Under 1,000 pre-Next-Fest wishlists is the "nobody knows you" tier. Use as a signal only.
- **Discord or TestFlight playtests.** 20–50 testers; at least 30% unprompted return; watch whether testers *choose* the timed mode.
- **Soft launch.** 2026 playbooks quote D1 of 35–40% and D7 of 15% as go gates and $10–30K a month of test spend in Canada, Australia and New Zealand, which is unaffordable here. Floors that matter with organic traffic: D1 of 25% or better is the top-quartile floor; D7 of 7–8% is top quartile. Puzzle-genre reference: 32 / 12 / 5.

## 6. Risks specific to this design

- **Word lists.** SCOWL is permissive (commercial use and resale allowed with the notice retained). ENABLE is public domain. WordNet is free for commercial use with attribution. Avoid the NASPA Word List and Collins Scrabble Words (proprietary). Raw SCOWL and ENABLE include slurs and obscure junk; NASPA removed 259 slurs in 2020 and reinstated over 100 in 2023. Expect to curate both a blocklist and an allowlist.
- **Crossword clues at scale.** Human rates: NYT $500–750 per weekday puzzle; LA Times $85–150; USA Today $100–125. The xd.saul.pw archive exposes 6M+ historical clue usages, but clues are publisher copyright: reference only, never ship. No credible study on AI-written clue quality was found. What is documented is that players accept curated, edited clues and find uncurated AI clues flat; Puzzmo and the NYT differentiate on editorial voice.
- **Anagram moderation.** Anagrams of caught letters will form slurs and obscenities (Alphabear's did, and they were shared for that reason). A profanity and slur blocklist over Babel's output is mandatory for App Store featuring and for Google Play's rule that AI features must not generate offensive content.
- **AI-made game backlash and store policy, 2025–2026.**
  - Player sentiment: Quantic Foundry, Oct–Dec 2025, n=1,799: 85% negative on generative AI in games, 63% picking the most negative option. Least negative for dynamic difficulty, most negative for art, music, narrative and dialogue. Negativity toward AI dialogue rose from 46% (2024) to 83% (2025).
  - Steam: 22% of 2025 releases disclosed AI, about 31% by July 2026. Rules from 16 January 2026 narrow disclosure to *player-facing* output; code and tools are exempt.
  - itch.io: mandatory generative-AI disclosure tags; untagged AI assets are de-indexed.
  - Google Play: in-app labels for user-facing generative-AI output required from 15 April 2026, extended to third-party AI APIs on 15 July 2026; "low-value" AI apps face removal.
  - Apple: guideline 5.1.2(i) (November 2025) requires explicit consent before sending personal data to third-party AI providers; the June 2026 update adds AI and quality language.
  - **Implication:** AI *coding* agents are invisible to players and exempt on Steam. AI-generated *art, music, story text or runtime Babel dialogue* is where the backlash and labelling bite. If Babel's anagrams come from a deterministic algorithm over authored templates, not from a language model at runtime, they are not "generative AI" under any of these policies. Keep it that way, or label it.

## 7. What the evidence says works

1. A free, instant, daily, web-playable core with a spoiler-free share card (Wordle, Puzzmo, Clues by Sam, Bracket City).
2. Zero-friction onboarding and calm pacing, with timers opt-in only (NYT, LinkedIn, SpellTower+).
3. A designer's name on the product plus App Store editorial relationships (Gage): a small team's realistic "distribution".
4. A five-second visual hook for short-form video. AstroLex's zero-G 3D letters are unusual enough to be that hook.
5. Being acquirable or licensable by a media company as a plausible outcome (NYT, Hearst, The Atlantic, Apple News+).
6. Curated word lists with an editorial voice. Anything that reads as an uncurated dictionary dump is punished by word-game audiences.

## 8. What tends to fail

1. Real-time or competitive word mechanics as the core (Babble Royale; competition motivation collapses after 35).
2. Reach without revenue: 10M-player word games that cannot fund a team (Alphabear, twice).
3. Cold premium or unlock launches on mobile with no prior brand. No positive example was found; Balatro's success rode 2M prior sales.
4. Steam as a revenue channel for puzzle and word games.
5. Betting on Netflix or Apple Arcade as launch channels.
6. Visible AI-generated art, story or dialogue in 2026.
7. Targeting 27–40 exclusively while designing against the 35+ female cohort that actually pays for word games.

## 9. Honest gaps

- No public revenue or conversion data for Knotwords, SpellTower+, Words in Progress, Typoman, Blossom, or any free-to-start word game.
- No word-genre-specific retention benchmark published since 2022.
- No measured share-rate data beyond Wordle's 2021–22 tweets.
- No credible study on AI-written crossword clue quality.
- LinkedIn's "3.5M DAU" and vendor "800% featuring uplift" figures are unaudited.
